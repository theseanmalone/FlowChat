Inside the Book

Chapter Highlights

Chapter 1
The Myth of the Accidental CTO
Most CTOs didn't plan to become CTOs. They were the best engineer, and then suddenly they had reports. This chapter reframes the role: the job isn't engineering, it's outcomes — and the translation function between business and technology.
Todd Persen (Hydrolix) · Jonathan LaCour (Mission Cloud)
Chapter 5
The Nearshore Advantage (and the Nearshore Trap)
Done right, nearshore isn't about cost — it's about time-zone alignment, cultural proximity, and world-class talent. Done wrong, it's cheap offshore in a nearshore wrapper. The difference is entirely in how you lead.
Chad Smykay (HPE) · Dom Scandinaro (Cameo)
Chapter 8
Product and Engineering Are Not the Same Team
Product measures outcomes. Engineering measures outputs. When those reward systems diverge, the teams diverge. One roadmap, shared ownership, and engineering at the table earlier — that's the fix.
Jeff Prince, CTO of XOi — Episode 171
Chapter 11
AI Is Not the Strategy
Companies with "AI strategies" often end up with impressive-sounding initiatives that don't connect to customer outcomes. AI is a capability. Start with the workflow, not the model. Find the human hours a machine can replace.
Chad Smykay (HPE) · Sylvain Kalache (Rootly AI)
Chapter 12
AI-Native Products Are Different
Not a traditional platform with AI bolted on — AI-native means the data model, UX, and business logic are designed around AI capabilities. Data quality becomes existential. The feedback loop is the product.
William Steenbergen, CTO of Federato · Behnam Bastani (OpenInfer)
Chapter 13
Security Is Not Someone Else's Job
Software supply chain attacks are here now. AI slop squatting is real. SBOM, shift-left testing, zero trust, and — most importantly — a culture where every engineer owns security, not just the security team.
Michael Lieberman (Kusari) · Jason Birmingham (Broadridge)

"The technology is almost never the problem. The problem is almost always clarity, trust, alignment, culture, or courage."

— Steve Taplin, Uncensored: Conclusion

Steve Taplin built 30 companies. Twenty of them failed. The pattern recognition from those failures — and the ten that succeeded — is the foundation of everything he teaches.

As CEO of Sonatafy Technology, Steve has pioneered the nearshore software development model, connecting elite Latin American engineering talent with U.S. companies in a way that delivers time-zone alignment, cultural proximity, and genuinely world-class output.

When he launched Software Leaders Uncensored in May 2025, he had one goal: to have the conversations that actually matter — not the polished keynote version, but the real talk that happens when experienced leaders tell each other what they wish someone had told them earlier.

188 episodes later, those conversations became this book.

Steve has been featured in Forbes, Entrepreneur Magazine, and recognized by CIO Today as one of the Top 100 Influential Tech Executives of 2023. He participated in the IBM Executive Development Program — an honor awarded to fewer than 1% of IBM's global workforce.
